\textbf{Improved watermark detectability.}
Recent work~\citep{he2026improving} introduces a second pseudo-random key to control
residual sampling and improve the trade-off under their watermark-strength metric.
However, detection remains subtle in speculative decoding because the detector does
not observe whether a token was produced by the draft path or by residual target
sampling. PFR avoids this ambiguity: every accepted output token is verified through
the target-side Poisson race, so the detector can score the realized sequence using
the same target-keyed randomness. Empirically
(Figure~\ref{fig:drafter_invariance}, left),
this leads to higher TPR@1\%FPR and close to the autoregressive watermark upper bound,
while preserving both watermark strength and speculative decoding efficiency.

\paragraph{Ablation studies}
We test whether \textsc{PFR}'s watermark signal is stable to drafter substitution.
Keeping the target model and watermark key fixed, we vary the drafter by model scale
(Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct $\to$ Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct) and sampling temperature
($T_{\mathrm{drafter}}\in\{0.5,1.0,1.5\}$). Figure~\ref{fig:drafter_invariance}
(right) shows TPR@1\%FPR across the resulting four drafter conditions.
\textsc{PFR} maintains a drift $\Delta\le 1$ percentage point at
$T_{\mathrm{eval}}\in\{64,128\}$, while \textsc{mse} drifts by $12$--$22$ pp
and \textsc{mse\_pseudo} (algorithm~1~\citep{he2026improving})
by $6$--$11$ pp across drafter conditions.
This indicates that PFR's target-side Poisson-race verification is largely insensitive
to the proposal mechanism. We further verify that output quality remains stable:
LPPL varies by at most $0.02$ and ROUGE-L by at most $0.005$ across evaluated decoders;
full tables are deferred to Appendix~\ref{app:ablation}.

\subsection{Ablation Details}
\label{app:ablation}
\paragraph{Drafter conditions.}
$D_0$: Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct at $T=1.0$ (default).
$D_1$: Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct at $T=1.0$ ($3\!\times$ scale, model swap).
$D_2$: Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct at $T=0.5$ (sharper drafter).
$D_3$: Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct at $T=1.5$ (more diffuse drafter).
Target temperature, watermark key, and all other parameters are held
fixed across $D_0$--$D_3$.
\paragraph{Detection statistic.}
For Aaronson Gamma-tail , per-token score
$s_t = -\log(1 - U_t)$ where $U_t$ is the per-token uniform recovered
from the watermark code. Under the no-watermark null, the cumulative
score $S_T = \sum_{t \le T} s_t$ is $\mathrm{Gamma}(T, 1)$; we declare
detection at FPR $=\alpha$ when $\Pr_{H_0}[\mathrm{Gamma}(T,1)\!>\!S_T]
\le \alpha$, computed via the regularised upper incomplete gamma. In this paper, we report as $\alpha =1\%$

\paragraph{Pairwise ROUGE-L.}
For each prompt $p$ and decoder $d$ we have one realised output token
sequence per drafter condition. We compute ROUGE-L F1 over each pair
$(D_i, D_j)$ of drafter conditions on these token sequences, then
report the per-prompt minimum over the six pairs (worst pair) averaged
across prompts (Table~\ref{tab:exp2_pairwise_rouge}). An unbiased
verify rule that depends only on target logits and key produces
identical sequences across all drafters, so this quantity should
approach $1$.
