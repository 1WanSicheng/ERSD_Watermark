\subsection{Ablation Studies}
\label{sec:ablation}

We test two claims about \textsc{PFR}/\textsc{MPFR}:
(i)~output quality is preserved, and (ii)~detection and the realised
output remain stable when the drafter is replaced. Both ablations use
Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (target) on \textsc{cnn\_dailymail}; full setup
and per-decoder tables are in App.~\ref{app:ablation}.

\paragraph{Output quality.}
Across nine schemes (\textsc{PFR}, \textsc{MPFR}, and seven baselines)
the spread on log-perplexity under the target and on ROUGE-L against
the gold summary is within sampling noise
($\Delta\textsc{lppl}\le 0.04$, $\Delta\textsc{rouge-l}\le 0.01$;
Table~\ref{tab:exp1_quality}). \textsc{PFR} matches the
no-watermark speedup baseline (\textsc{mc}) to within $0.001$ on
\textsc{lppl} while delivering $2.44\times$ AATPS; \textsc{MPFR}
delivers $3.09\times$.

\paragraph{Drafter substitution.}
We perturb the drafter along two axes — model scale
(Qwen2.5-0.5B\,$\to$\,1.5B) and temperature ($T_\mathrm{drafter}\in
\{0.5,1.0,1.5\}$) — yielding four conditions $D_0$--$D_3$ with the
target and key held fixed. Fig.~\ref{fig:drafter_invariance} shows TPR
at FPR $=1\%$ (Aaronson Gamma-tail \citep{aaronson2023watermark}) as a
function of the detection token budget $T_{\mathrm{eval}}$, per drafter
condition. For an unbiased verify rule the four drafter curves
overlap; for a biased verify rule they fan out. \textsc{PFR} (ours)
and \textsc{mws} maintain drift $\Delta\le 2\,\mathrm{pp}$ across the
full evaluation range, whereas \textsc{mse} and Algorithm~1
\citep{he2026watermarked} lose $15$--$23\,\mathrm{pp}$ under the most
modest perturbation ($D_0\!\to\!D_2$). Because \textsc{PFR}'s verify
rule depends only on the target's logits and the watermark key,
detection invariance further implies sequence invariance: the same
prompt yields near-identical token sequences across drafters
(per-decoder pairwise ROUGE-L in App.~Table~\ref{tab:exp2_pairwise_rouge}).

\paragraph{Implication.}
In any deployment that may substitute the drafter — cost-driven
distillation swaps, multi-tenant serving with heterogeneous draft
engines, or routine drafter fine-tunes — only an unbiased verify rule
gives a stable detection guarantee. \textsc{mse} and Algorithm~1
silently lose $\ge 15\,\mathrm{pp}$ of TPR, an effect invisible to a
paper-time evaluation that fixes the drafter.

\section{Ablation Details}
\label{app:ablation}

\paragraph{Setup.}
Both ablations use Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct as the target on
\textsc{cnn\_dailymail} with $\textrm{top-}k=50$, $T_\mathrm{target}=1$,
$\texttt{max\_new\_tokens}=128$, lookahead $K=4$, $n=1000$ prompts,
single seed; runs on a single A100. The watermark key is fixed across
all decoders that use one. The default drafter is Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct.

\paragraph{Drafter conditions (Exp~2).}
$D_0$: Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct at $T=1.0$ (default).
$D_1$: Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct at $T=1.0$ ($3\!\times$ scale, model swap).
$D_2$: Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct at $T=0.5$ (sharper drafter).
$D_3$: Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct at $T=1.5$ (more diffuse drafter).
Target temperature, watermark key, and all other parameters are held
fixed across $D_0$--$D_3$.

\paragraph{Detection statistic.}
For Aaronson Gamma-tail \citep{aaronson2023watermark}, per-token score
$s_t = -\log(1 - U_t)$ where $U_t$ is the per-token uniform recovered
from the watermark code. Under the no-watermark null, the cumulative
score $S_T = \sum_{t \le T} s_t$ is $\mathrm{Gamma}(T, 1)$; we declare
detection at FPR $=\alpha$ when $\Pr_{H_0}[\mathrm{Gamma}(T,1)\!>\!S_T]
\le \alpha$, computed via the regularised upper incomplete gamma.

\paragraph{Pairwise ROUGE-L (Exp~2).}
For each prompt $p$ and decoder $d$ we have one realised output token
sequence per drafter condition. We compute ROUGE-L F1 over each pair
$(D_i, D_j)$ of drafter conditions on these token sequences, then
report the per-prompt minimum over the six pairs (worst pair) averaged
across prompts (Table~\ref{tab:exp2_pairwise_rouge}). An unbiased
verify rule that depends only on target logits and key produces
identical sequences across all drafters, so this quantity should
approach $1$.
